# Research findings: AI theater and the Agile parallel

**Bottom line:** The data overwhelmingly supports the thesis. Across every major consultancy report from 2024–2026, the same finding repeats: **70% of AI implementation challenges stem from people and process issues, not technology** (BCG). Companies that redesign workflows are **2.8× more likely** to see real financial impact (McKinsey). Yet most organizations continue treating AI as a procurement decision rather than an organizational transformation — a pattern that mirrors the Agile movement's dilution almost exactly.

---

## Section 1: The problem framing — why "better, faster, cheaper" fails

### AI project failure rates (2024–2026)

The failure statistics are stark and well-documented across multiple authoritative sources:

- **80% of AI projects fail** — double the rate of traditional IT projects. RAND Corporation, 2024, based on interviews with 65 experienced AI practitioners. The report identified misaligned incentives and absence of end-user co-design as the highest-impact failure causes.
- **74% of companies struggle to achieve and scale value from AI.** Only **4% consistently generate significant value** across functions. BCG, "Where's the Value in AI?," October 2024, survey of 1,000 CxOs across 59 countries.
- **Only 6% of organizations are "AI high performers"** (attributing >5% of EBIT to AI). **88% of organizations use AI** in at least one function, but only **39% attribute any EBIT impact** to it. McKinsey, "The State of AI," March 2025.
- **At least 50% of GenAI projects were abandoned** after proof of concept by end of 2025. Gartner, updated finding from an initial prediction of 30%.
- **42% of companies abandoned most of their AI initiatives in 2025**, up from 17% in 2024. S&P Global Market Intelligence, survey of 1,000+ respondents.
- **57% of I&O leaders who reported failures said they expected too much, too fast.** Only **28% of AI use cases fully succeed and meet ROI expectations.** Gartner survey of 782 I&O leaders, November–December 2025.
- **95% of generative AI pilots fail** to deliver measurable P&L impact, per MIT's "GenAI Divide" report (July 2025, lead author Aditya Challapally). Note: this figure has been questioned by some analysts; the actual success rate among those who attempted AI projects may be closer to 8.3%.

### The cost-reduction trap

**Nigel Vaz, CEO of Publicis Sapient** (Fortune, January 15, 2026): *"You should be looking for butterflies — not faster caterpillars. The definition of transformation is exactly that — caterpillars becoming butterflies. Otherwise, you are not recognizing AI's potential."* He added: *"Many CEOs have been focused on cost-based use cases of AI, because it's easier to prove their tangible value."*

**BCG's 10-20-70 principle** quantifies the problem: AI success is **10% algorithms, 20% data and technology, and 70% people, processes, and cultural transformation**. Yet most investment flows toward the 30%.

**HBR** (February 2026, Sangeet Paul Choudary): *"Many incumbent companies are adopting AI aggressively yet seeing only marginal gains, because they are using it to optimize existing work rather than to rethink how work itself is organized."*

### Misaligned incentives in practice

**HBR** (March 2026, Karim Lakhani, Jared Spataro, Jen Stave): Identified seven structural frictions in AI adoption, including the "efficiency trap." Key quote: *"When companies frame AI purely as a cost-reduction tool, it triggers defensive responses from middle management and narrows executive ambition."* They also found: *"What remains scarce is the leadership ability to imagine and commit to a different way of running the enterprise."*

**Beverly Wright** (decision sciences pioneer): *"Companies announce AI initiatives to move a stock price, and the announcement becomes the goal."*

**McKinsey** (2025): Employees are **3× more likely to be using gen AI** than their leaders estimate (13% vs. 4%), revealing a profound disconnect between leadership perception and reality.

**Deloitte TrustID data** (via HBR, November 2025): Trust in company-provided gen AI among frontline workers **fell 31%** between May–July 2025. Trust in agentic AI systems **dropped 89%** during the same period.

## Section 2: The Agile parallel — concrete evidence

### Origins of "Agile theater"

**Daniel Mezick** coined "Agile Industrial Complex" in December 2016: *"It is that web of Agile institutions, Agile thought leaders and Agile consulting firms that implicitly collude to make normal the very harmful and disrespectful imposition of Agile practices on teams without consent."*

**Martin Fowler** (Agile Manifesto signatory) amplified the concept at Agile Australia 2018: *"The Agile Industrial Complex imposing methods on people is an absolute travesty."*

### Agile pioneers' backlash — direct quotes

**Dave Thomas** (Agile Manifesto signatory), "Agile is Dead (Long Live Agility)," March 2014: *"The word 'agile' has been subverted to the point where it is effectively meaningless, and what passes for an agile community seems to be largely an arena for consultants and vendors to hawk services and products."*

**Ron Jeffries** (Agile Manifesto signatory), "Developers Should Abandon Agile," 2018: *"When 'Agile' ideas are applied poorly, they often lead to more interference with developers, less time to do the work, higher pressure, and demands to 'go faster'. This is bad for the developers, and, ultimately, bad for the enterprise as well."* He coined the terms "Faux Agile" and "Dark Scrum" to describe abusive implementations.

**Robert C. Martin** ("Uncle Bob," Agile Manifesto signatory): *"The Agile message has gotten so badly twisted and torqued and perverted… Agile was a small idea for helping small teams do small things. It was not the overarching pattern of software development that was to dominate the world."* On the certification takeover: *"Ken Schwaber decided that he wanted to teach a course called the Certified Scrum Master course… Everybody wanted to be a Certified Scrum Master; not one programmer wanted to be a Certified Scrum Master. All the project managers wanted it. They flooded into Agile, they took over the message, they took over the conferences… and they literally pushed the programmers out."*

### Failed Agile transformation data

- **47% of Agile transformations fail**, per Jeff Sutherland (co-creator of Scrum).
- **42% of failures** were due to company philosophy/culture at odds with Agile values. **38%** from lack of management support. **36%** from lack of support for cultural transition. (VersionOne 9th Annual State of Agile Survey)
- **84% of organizations** acknowledge they are below a high level of Agile competency (State of Agile 2025).
- **Only 13%** of respondents in a KPMG survey reported that top management fully supports Agile transformation.
- The **State of Agile Culture Report** (2021–2022) found that while 68% of respondents said they felt empowered by leaders, neuroscience analysis determined **only 17% actually felt empowered** — a massive cognitive dissonance.

### The SAFe controversy as the symbol of dilution

**Marty Cagan** (Silicon Valley Product Group): *"I don't personally know of a single leading tech product company that is using SAFe."* He added: *"I went through and compared this list with SAFe, and literally all ten problems [of waterfall] exist in SAFe."*

**Maarten Dalmijn** captured the paradox: *"SAFe tells a story the CEOs of large companies like to hear: risk-free results without requiring significant changes."*

Even PMI's Disciplined Agile acknowledges: *"SAFe ends up being a better waterfall, where teams work in increments but the organization's agility doesn't improve significantly."*

### The pattern that maps to AI

The Agile trajectory followed a clear arc: organic origin (2001) → early success → commercialization via certifications and consulting → dilution as rituals replaced principles → backlash from founders (2014–2018). The core lesson: **treating a mindset shift as a product to be purchased, certified, and imposed top-down** — exactly what the Manifesto opposed — is precisely the pattern now emerging in AI adoption.

## Section 3: Workflow redesign — specific approaches and frameworks

### The data case for workflow redesign

**McKinsey** (2025): Workflow redesign had **the biggest effect** on an organization's ability to see EBIT impact from gen AI, out of 25 attributes tested. AI high performers were **2.8× more likely** to report fundamental workflow redesign (55% vs. 20%).

**Bain & Company**: Organizations that deliver on both workforce engagement and workflow modernization generate **TSR 2.3× that of companies** that don't.

**Accenture** (January 2024): Only **9% of organizations** qualify as "Reinventors" — leading in AI reinvention. Reinventors are **2× more likely** to anticipate productivity gains of 20%+. Three-quarters actively involve workers in redesigning their work and roles.

### Named frameworks

**BCG's 10-20-70 principle**: 10% algorithms, 20% data/technology, 70% people, processes, and cultural transformation. Provides a budget allocation framework.

**McKinsey's "Agentic Organization" model** (September 2025): Five pillars — business model, operating model, governance, workforce/culture, and technology/data. Key quote: *"Delegating the agentic transformation to your technology leader, as you would with a software deployment, will not suffice."*

**Accenture's "Total Enterprise Reinvention"**: Task-level analysis of nearly 300 tasks and 90 roles, matching tasks to AI agent capabilities. Envisions five interconnected workforces: human, human+machine collaboration, intelligent automation, generative AI, and agentic AI. **84% of executives** plan to redesign roles and teams around AI agents within the next few years.

**MIT EPOCH methodology** (Loaiza & Rigobon, California Management Review, July 2025): Measures jobs/tasks across five dimensions — Empathy, Presence, Opinion, Creativity, Hope — and identifies four strategic approaches: Status Quo, Augmentation-led, Human-in-the-Loop, and Displacement-Driven.

**Bain's "Four High-Gain Moves"**: (1) Deploy AI in a human-centric way; (2) Address accumulated "workflow debt"; (3) Reimagine employee value proposition; (4) Build perpetual productivity engines. Case study: a leading global bank redesigned workflow and roles together, compressing a **60–100 day process with 10+ handoffs into a one-day cycle**.

### AI-native vs. bolted-on process design

**IBM's definition**: A system qualifies as AI-native only when AI is so central that removing it would make the product cease to function — not just perform worse, but become useless.

**The "bolt test"** (practical diagnostic): If you see buttons labeled "Launch AI" or "Open AI Assistant" on the interface, you're looking at bolt-on AI, not AI-native design.

**Scott Rodgers, Deloitte CTO** (MIT Technology Review/Deloitte, April 2026): *"You need to shift the operating model to humans as governors and agents as operators."* He warned: *"The real risk isn't that AI won't work — it's that competitors will redesign their operating models while you're still piloting agents and copilots."*

### Human-in-the-loop in practice

Two failure modes exist: over-automation (deploying fully autonomous AI for judgment tasks) and under-integration (layering AI onto manual processes and calling it an upgrade). Effective HITL design requires clear escalation paths, context preservation during handoffs, confidence thresholds, and error routing.

## Section 4: Culture and incentive changes — concrete examples

### Incentive restructuring

**Wharton framework** (Scott Snyder): Three-level incentive structure — leader level (tie compensation to AI outcomes), team level (share AI tools and deliverables), individual level (reward experimentation). Companies cited: **Walmart and Pfizer** use bonus multipliers tied to AI-driven KPIs; **Amazon and JPMorgan** link promotions to AI adoption metrics.

**McKinsey** (December 2025): *"Instead of offering bonuses for implementing AI, successful organizations reward employees for demonstrating new competencies, sharing insights with colleagues, and helping others navigate the learning curve."* Shift the question from "How is the AI project going?" to "What did you learn that surprised you?"

**Team-level adoption** is key: *"One champion on a team is worth 10 mandates from the top"* (Time Magazine/Charter synthesis). **Foursquare** shifted from individual to team-based performance evaluations. **Atlassian** tracks which teams have embedded champions as a leading indicator of adoption.

### Psychological safety

**HBR** (February 2026, Eatough, Ferrazzi, Smith): *"AI adoption stalls because employees' industry-shaped anxiety about relevance, identity, and job security drives surface-level use without real commitment. Leaders who treat AI adoption as a psychological and contextual challenge — not just a technical rollout — are far more likely to convert experimentation into sustained impact."*

**McKinsey** (October 2024): Recommends starting with low-risk experimentation — drafting emails, searching for travel options — to demystify the technology and build comfort before tackling core workflows.

A critical finding: workers fail to report time savings from AI due to fear of job loss or being asked to do more with less. This creates a hidden feedback loop that undermines measurement and scaling.

### Upskilling programs at scale

- **Amazon "Upskilling 2025"**: $1.2B invested, 350,000+ employees trained, 100,000+ moved into higher-skilled roles.
- **JPMorgan**: $600M annual training budget; mandatory AI literacy for all 300,000 employees; segmented training by four audiences (general, technologists, data scientists, executives). 40% reduction in manual reporting tasks.
- **Microsoft Elevate** (July 2025): $4B initiative aiming to credential 20 million people in AI within two years.
- **PwC "New World, New Skills"**: $3B program training all 275,000 employees in AI basics.
- **BCG's "Five Must-Haves for AI Upskilling"** (October 2024): Assess needs, prepare workers for change, introduce incentives, position C-suite at forefront, use AI tools with network effects.

### Leadership as the bottleneck

**McKinsey** (2025): *"The true barrier isn't employee readiness but a lack of leadership alignment on AI strategy, investment, and risk management."* Leaders are identified as **the greatest barrier** to AI adoption.

BCG found **62% of C-suite executives** cited talent shortage as their biggest challenge — yet **only 6% had begun upskilling their workforce in a meaningful way**. This gap between recognizing the problem and acting on it mirrors the Agile pattern precisely.

## Section 5: Case studies of companies ahead in AI adoption

### Klarna — the cautionary tale

**What happened:** Klarna launched an OpenAI-powered AI assistant in January 2024 that handled **2.3 million conversations in its first month** — two-thirds of all customer service chats, across 23 markets and 35+ languages. The AI did the equivalent work of **700 full-time agents**. Customer service costs per transaction dropped **40%** ($0.32 → $0.19). Workforce shrank from 5,527 (end 2022) to ~3,000.

**What went wrong:** By May 2025, CEO Sebastian Siemiatkowski publicly admitted the approach had gone too far. *"Cost unfortunately seems to have been a too predominant evaluation factor… what you end up having is lower quality. Really investing in the quality of the human support is the way of the future for us."* Customer satisfaction declined on complex cases. Escalation handling suffered. Institutional knowledge gaps appeared.

**The pivot:** Klarna began rehiring human agents, adopted a hybrid model (AI handles routine Tier-1; humans handle complex/emotional issues), and acknowledged it needed an "Uber-type" workforce model. **Forrester analyst Kate Leggett**: *"They overpivoted to cost containment, without thinking about the longer-term impact of customer experience."*

**Key lesson:** Klarna became the **canonical enterprise cautionary tale** for AI-as-replacement versus AI-as-augmentation. No retraining programs were offered. No workforce transition support was communicated. The 96% daily AI usage among remaining employees is impressive, but the quality degradation forced a costly reversal.

### JPMorgan Chase — enterprise scale with cultural intentionality

**Scale:** $18B annual tech budget; $1.3B specifically on AI (2024). **200,000+ employees** using LLM Suite; roughly half of JPMorgan's 317,000-person workforce uses GenAI daily. **450+ AI use cases in production.**

**Key systems:** COiN (Contract Intelligence) processes 12,000 credit agreements annually in seconds — work that previously consumed **360,000 lawyer-hours per year**. LLM Suite is model-agnostic (integrates OpenAI and Anthropic), updates every 8 weeks, and won American Banker's 2025 Innovation of the Year Grand Prize.

**Cultural approach:** The bank used an **opt-in adoption strategy** — LLM Suite was not mandated but made available, creating "healthy competition" and FOMO dynamics that onboarded 100,000+ employees within months. Training was segmented by four audiences: general employees ("AI Made Easy"), technologists, data scientists, and executives. Peer-driven learning included prompt libraries and "prompt of the week" emails.

**Derek Waldron** (Chief Analytics Officer): *"Most employees would say 2024 was the year they developed a personal relationship with AI. I would describe it as nothing short of a cultural transformation for the bank."*

**Jamie Dimon** (2024 shareholder letter): *"We already have huge redeployment plans for our own people… we have displaced people from AI — and we offer them other jobs."* AI was his #1 topic.

**Key lesson:** Opt-in adoption beats mandates; security-first works even in heavily regulated industries; redeployment plans preserve trust.

### Moderna — culture-first AI transformation

**Scale:** ~3,000–6,000 employees. Partnered with OpenAI; deployed ChatGPT Enterprise. Within two months: **750 custom GPTs** created company-wide, **40% of weekly active users** created their own GPTs, average of **120 ChatGPT Enterprise conversations per user per week**. Legal team achieved **100% adoption**.

**How they did it:** Moderna spent a decade building a digital-first foundation before deploying enterprise AI tools. CEO **Stéphane Bancel** personally uses ChatGPT daily and set a target: employees should use AI no fewer than **20 times per day**. The company held AI prompt contests to identify the top 100 power users, who became "Generative AI Champions" — an internal cohort spreading AI skills. An internal AI forum had **2,000 weekly participants**.

**Bancel**: *"If we had to do it the old biopharmaceutical ways, we might need 100,000 people. Today, the company is less than 6,000 people. We really believe we can maximize our impact on patients with a few thousand people by using technology and AI to scale the company."*

**Brad Miller** (CIO): *"90% of companies want to do GenAI, but only 10% of them are successful, and the reason they fail is because they haven't built the mechanisms of actually transforming the workforce to adopt new technology and new capabilities."*

**Key lesson:** Culture-first + CEO evangelism + bottom-up innovation (employees creating their own GPTs) + augmentation framing (not replacement) enables extraordinary adoption rates.

### GitHub/Microsoft — Copilot and developer culture shifts

**Adoption:** **20 million+ all-time users** by July 2025; **4.7 million paid subscribers** (75% YoY growth); **90% of Fortune 100** deployed Copilot; **42% market share** among paid AI coding tools. Copilot is now a **larger business than GitHub was** when Microsoft acquired it for $7.5B.

**Productivity data:** Controlled experiments showed **55% faster task completion** (~4,800 developers). A field experiment across Microsoft and Accenture found **26% increase in completed tasks**. Pull request time dropped from **9.6 days to 2.4 days** (Opsera research). Copilot generates **46% of all code** written by active users (up from 27% at launch).

**Workflow changes:** Developers shifted from coding from scratch to reviewing and directing AI-generated code — the role is becoming more "orchestration" than "implementation." An **11-week ramp-up period** was identified before developers fully realize benefits. **80% license utilization** when tools are simply made available — suggesting strong organic adoption without mandates.

**Cultural impact:** **60–75% of developers** felt more fulfilled and less frustrated. **84% of corporate users say they wouldn't go back** to working without Copilot. **Satya Nadella** drove Microsoft's culture from "know-it-all to learn-it-all," which he credits for the company's growth from $300B to ~$3 trillion market cap.

### Shopify — the AI-first mandate

**Tobi Lütke** posted a 1,300-word internal memo publicly (April 2025): *"Reflexive AI usage is now a baseline expectation at Shopify."* and *"Before asking for more headcount and resources, teams must demonstrate why they cannot get what they want done using AI."* AI competency became part of **performance reviews and hiring decisions**.

**Implementation:** CTO ordered 1,500 Cursor licenses, quickly had to procure another 1,500. Fastest-growing AI tool users were **not engineers** — it was support and revenue teams. No spending quotas on AI tokens. An internal **leaderboard tracks who's spending the most on AI tokens**. Weekly "f-r-A-I-days" for experimentation. Legal was a key enabler, not blocker.

**Notably:** Shopify is hiring **more** interns, not fewer — because interns *"are the ones who are using AI in the most interesting ways."* Scaled intern program from 25 to a planned 1,000.

### Walmart — operational AI at scale

**Scale:** "My Assistant" GenAI tool rolled out to **2.1 million associates**. "Ask Sam" AI assistant used by **900,000 associates** who ask **over 3 million questions per week**. ML-powered route optimization saved **30 million unnecessary driving miles**. Fashion production timelines reduced by **up to 18 weeks**.

**Proprietary approach:** Built **Wallaby** (retail-specific LLMs trained on decades of proprietary data) and **Element** (MLOps platform). Philosophy is "people-led, tech-powered." CEO Doug McMillon: *"There is a native AI experience coming that is multi-media, personalized and contextual. We are running towards that."*

### Amazon — developer transformation at massive scale

**Key result:** Migrated **30,000 production applications** from Java 8/11 to Java 17 using Amazon Q code transformation. Average upgrade time: from **50 developer-days to a few hours per application**. Saved equivalent of **4,500 developer-years of work** and **$260 million annually**. **79% of AI-generated code reviews shipped without additional changes.**

**Andy Jassy** (2025 shareholder letter): *"AI is a once-in-a-lifetime opportunity where the current growth is unprecedented and the future growth even bigger. We're not going to be conservative in how we play this."* Plans **$200B in AI infrastructure spending for 2026**.

**Workforce impact:** Plans to cut ~14,000 corporate roles (~4%). Jassy urged employees to *"figure out how to get more done with scrappier teams."*

## Section 6: A practical roadmap — sequencing, milestones, warning signs

### Why culture → workflow → tooling (not the reverse)

**Bain**: *"If technology's impact on people is treated as a downstream change management challenge, AI won't get past micro-productivity improvements. Companies will settle for less ambitious goals, less creative technology deployment, and lower adoption and scaling."*

**HBS Professor Karim Lakhani**: *"Scaling AI successfully requires more than advanced technology. Sustained impact relies on transforming culture — aligning leadership, nurturing new skills, building trust, and supporting ongoing adoption."*

**McKinsey's "Agentic Organization" model** (September 2025) explicitly warns against delegating transformation to the technology leader alone.

### Realistic milestones (12–18 month phased roadmap)

Based on synthesis across McKinsey, Bain, Gartner, and Accenture:

1. **Assessment (months 1–2):** Audit data, infrastructure, and personnel. Identify workflow debt. Build leadership alignment. Define measurable objectives.
2. **Strategy and prioritization (months 2–4):** Select and sequence high-impact use cases. Workforce planning. Governance framework. Culture/change management kickoff.
3. **Foundation building (months 3–6):** Address workflow debt. Data quality programs. Establish Centers of Excellence. Initial upskilling. Create psychological safety for experimentation.
4. **Pilot launch (months 4–8):** Focused pilots in high-impact, low-risk areas with clear hypotheses. Embed human-in-the-loop. Redesign workflows — not just layer AI on.
5. **Scaling (months 8–14):** Expand successful pilots. Embed AI in core workflows. Redesign roles and job descriptions. Overhaul performance metrics.
6. **Continuous operations (month 14+):** Ongoing optimization. Model monitoring. Continuous learning programs. Governance refinement.

**McKinsey** notes companies attributing 5%+ EBIT to AI share common traits: bold ambitions, redesigned workflows, faster scaling, and significantly higher investment.

### Warning signs of "AI theater"

These are the diagnostic indicators that an organization is performing AI adoption rather than doing it:

- **Measuring adoption, not outcomes:** Tracking licenses, logins, or vague usage rather than business impact.
- **Disconnected pilots:** 500 use cases that don't talk to each other. **IDC** finding: of every 33 AI proofs-of-concept a company launches, only **4 make it into production**.
- **Plateaued adoption:** Same 20 power users generating 80%+ of usage; frozen use cases; fading executive attention.
- **Tool fatigue without workflow change:** Adding AI tools on top of existing processes without redesigning how work gets done.
- **Renaming roles without changing responsibilities:** Like Agile theater's renaming of project managers as "Scrum Masters" without changing decision-making authority.
- **Pilots that never mature:** AI theater thrives in pilots and prototypes that never scale.
- **"Agent washing":** Gartner (June 2025) warned of vendors rebranding existing RPA and chatbots as "agents" without substantial agentic capabilities. Over **40% of agentic AI projects will be canceled by end of 2027**.

### The parallel in one line

**Jeff Bezos** (quoted in Marty Cagan's SAFe critique): *"If you're not watchful, the process can become the thing. You stop looking at outcomes and just make sure you're doing the process right."* This is the essence of both Agile theater and AI theater — and the clearest warning sign that a transformation has become a performance.

## Conclusion: the synthesis

The research paints a consistent picture across every major consultancy and research institution. **Adoption is nearly universal; value creation is rare.** Between 78–88% of companies use AI, but only 4–6% generate significant value (McKinsey, BCG). The root cause is organizational, not technological — 70% of challenges are people- and process-related (BCG). The winning formula requires workflow redesign before tool selection, culture change before workflow redesign, and leadership commitment before everything.

The Agile parallel is not merely metaphorical — it is structural. Both movements started with genuine insights from practitioners, were commercialized into frameworks and certifications, diluted by organizations seeking results without change, and eventually prompted backlash from their founders. The question is whether AI adoption will learn from this history or repeat it. The early data — 80% failure rates, 50%+ abandonment after proof of concept, and the Klarna reversal — suggests the pattern is already well underway.

The companies pulling ahead (Moderna, JPMorgan, Shopify) share three traits: CEO-led cultural transformation, bottom-up experimentation with top-down vision, and framing AI as augmentation rather than replacement. The companies stumbling (Klarna's initial approach, the median enterprise with 33 POCs and 4 in production) share one: they treated AI as a tool to be purchased rather than a way of working to be learned.
